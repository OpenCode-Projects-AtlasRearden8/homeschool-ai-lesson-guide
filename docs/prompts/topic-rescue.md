# Prompt: Topic Rescue

*Category: teaching · Use: when a concept isn't landing or a lesson fell
apart · Pairs with: chapters 09 and 10.*

## When to reach for this

- Third explanation of the same concept, and your kid's eyes have glaze
- The planned activity is impossible today (materials, weather, mood)
- You realize *you* don't fully get what you're about to teach

Rescue prompts are the highest-value use of AI in homeschooling, because a
model can generate six alternate teaching angles in one minute — and one of
them usually lands.

## Paste this prompt

```text
I am teaching [CONCEPT] to [descriptor: age, level, what they anchor to —
interests, what they already understand]. The standard textbook explanation
isn't landing after two tries. What I know about how they think: [e.g., "they
think in stories and pictures; abstract definitions stall them"].

Give me SIX different angles on this concept, each in 3 sentences max and
labeled:

1. A story analogy (narrative-first, human-scale)
2. A physical demo with objects I likely own (kitchen/backyard; nothing I
   must order)
3. A question-first approach (lead them to discover the idea)
4. A drawing/diagram they produce, not one I show
5. A wrong-then-right approach (show a charming misconception, fix it)
6. A "you be the teacher" version (they explain it to someone back)

Then, one short paragraph: which sequence of two angles usually works for
learners who think in [stories and pictures], and why.
```

## Rules for the output

- Verify any *factual* content in the six angles same as always (chapter 05)
  — analogies are especially prone to teaching wrong physics with charm.
- Try at most two angles per session; more, and it becomes a media choice
  game instead of a lesson.
- Log which angle landed (one line, in the kid's note or weekly log). After
  a quarter, your log is a personalized "what works for this kid" file —
  turn it into a reusable lead line in future prompts like the ones in
  [prompts/weekly-plan.md](weekly-plan.md).

## The quiet-variant sub-prompt (low energy days)

Same concept, no supplies, no printing, max 20 minutes:

```text
The lesson fell through today. Quiet 20-minute variant on [CONCEPT]: no
printing, no supplies beyond pencil and paper, no screen. Give me the
shortest version that still teaches the core idea, plus the one question I
should ask at the end to know it stuck.
```

# Portfolios and Logs

*Summary: one records tree, two documents you update weekly, and a quarterly
30-minute review. Your future self — and, if requested, your state — can
reconstruct the whole year in an afternoon. Time to set up: 30 minutes once.*

## Why keep records at all

Three separate reasons, and the order matters:

1. **Some states require it.** For those states, records are compliance
   documents. Chapter 07 helps you find what yours asks for.
2. **For assessment time.** Standardized tests, evaluators, or annual
   reviews are dramatically easier when materials exist, organized.
3. **For you.** The mid-February "are we actually learning anything" spiral
   is fixed by opening a portfolio. It's also where the fact-check trail,
   the AI-generated drafts, and the kid's actual work live — evidence that
   *you* designed the year, whatever a well-meaning relative thinks.

## The records tree

One folder, year-numbered, structure like this:

```
records/
  2026-2027/
    year-plan.md            copied from chapter 02
    weekly-log.md           running table (template below)
    fact-checks.md          verification trail (chapter 05)
    portfolio-index.md      the table of contents of the year
    weeks/
      week-01/
        plan.md             that week's actual plan (from the loop)
        worksheets/         print + save, or scan
        photos/             projects, experiments, models
        kid-work/           scanned or photographed real work
    assessments/
      quarter-1-notes.md
    correspondence/         letters to/from district, receipts, etc.
```

Homeschoolers who dread records have almost always kept them in too many
places. **One folder, one weekly-log file, one year, in order.**

## The two weekly documents

### 1. Weekly log (template: [templates/weekly-log.md](templates/weekly-log.md))

Five rows a week, written Sunday during the planning block in 5 minutes:

```markdown
| Kid | Subject | This week we | Evidence (file / photo / note) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sam | History | read ch. 6–7 of spine book; made timeline card | photos/timeline-01.jpg |
| Sam | Math | Saxon lessons 31–33 | scan-lesson33.pdf |
| Ivy | Reading | decode CVC words, 5/8 correct | worksheet wk04 |
```

Write the evidence column *immediately* — the habit that kills portfolios is
"I'll add evidence later." Later is never, so put the pointer in even if the
photo gets taken tomorrow.

### 2. Portfolio index (template: [templates/portfolio-index.md](templates/portfolio-index.md))

Updated roughly monthly in the same sitting as a quarterly review. It's the
table of contents: units completed, books read, projects, subject-by-subject
summary. A skeptical evaluator reads the index in two minutes and sees a
competent year. You read it in the same two minutes and feel like a
professional. Everyone wins.

## The quarterly review, 30 minutes, four times a year

Works best in weeks 12, 24, 36, and any week you take a break:

1. Skim the weekly log for the quarter. Underline the wins (yes, pen).
2. Update the portfolio index: units done? books finished? subjects lagging?
3. Screenshot the quarter's biggest project, best worksheet, and worst
   disaster — put all three in the quarter folder. Real years include
   disasters; evaluators know this; it also proves you're honest.
4. Reconcile the plan: is the year plan drifting? (Chapter 03's steps cover
   how to slide/compress; do it here while it's cheap.)

## What NOT to keep

- **Chat histories.** They expire, they're not portable, and they're
  clutter. Extract to files during the weekly loop (Move 4); delete the rest.
- **Every single worksheet.** Keep representative samples — one great, one
  typical, one hard day. A thousand-page portfolio isn't better. 50 good
  pages per kid per year is a *strong* portfolio.
- **Anything identifying a minor that could embarrass them in 10 years.**
  You are building an archive about your kid. Assume they'll read it
  someday. Write like the kindest teacher you ever had.

## If your state asks to see them

Breathe. Chapter 07 covers how to determine whether that's even a thing in
your state. If it is, the version of this portfolio you show is:

- the weekly log (dated, complete)
- the portfolio index
- samples, 3 per subject per quarter
- your correspondence folder

That's it. Being organized is legal in every state; the records here are
built so showing them is a 10-minute print job, not a life event.

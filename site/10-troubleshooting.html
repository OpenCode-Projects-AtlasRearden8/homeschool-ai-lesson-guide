Troubleshooting

Summary: the ten failure patterns you'll actually hit, and the fastest honest fix for each. Bookmark this one.

1. "Everything it generates sounds the same"

Generic output is a context problem, not a model problem. Your prompt needs three more specifics it doesn't have: your kid's actual interests, the materials you own, and a length constraint. "Use my kids' current obsession (nail polish/forestry/Bluey) as an example in at least one item" works shockingly well.

2. "It keeps giving me classroom-seat-time plans"

Because most of its training data is classroom. Correct it once, explicitly: "In our home school we do 3 hours a day, mixed ages at one table, no transitions, no 25-student group work." The model adapts immediately — this is a one-paragraph fix per prompt, or saved as a "preamble" you reuse (the prompt library files in prompts/ each contain one ready-made).

3. "A fact on today's worksheet is wrong"

Good catch — that's chapter 05 doing its job. Fix the sheet, and write the correction in your fact-checks.md with the correct date/name, so the fixed version gets used next time you regenerate the unit.

4. "The reading level came out wrong — too hard"

Two reliable moves: (1) ask for shorter sentences and 100 fewer unique words; (2) ask it to "explain the same content to someone reading it aloud to a listener who can stop and ask questions." Reading level is measurable; ask the model to report its sentence-length average if it can, and compare against your kid's real tolerance.

5. "My kid names it 'Siri-for-school' and refuses"

Rebrand. The AI is your planning tool, not an in-lesson presence. If your kid does interact with it (asking questions), set rules like "AI is for finding sources, then we check them" — that habit is chapter 05, taught early, and it's a genuinely good life skill.

6. "I fell a whole unit behind"

Chapter 03's slide-the-plan move, done once, is fifteen minutes and fixes the vibe immediately. Do not try to double-up lessons to "catch up" — doubling is how plans die in winter. If behind by a lot, use the AI to compress: "here's the rest of this unit's plan and my honest remaining weeks; propose what to cut and what to keep."

7. "It wrote a worksheet whose answer key is wrong"

This happens with generated math word problems more than anyone admits. Spot-check keys before Monday when the topic is arithmetic; if the key is wrong, fix it yourself, log it in fact-checks.md, and consider regenerating the whole sheet — broken keys mean the model was confused the whole way through.

8. "My records folder is chaos"

One-time triage day (30 min): archive everything not current into an archive-YYYY/ subfolder; keep this year's records/2026-2027/ clean. Then make the ritual stricter: Move 4 of the weekly loop exists precisely so chaos can't re-form. If paperwork from the state is mixed in, chapter 06's correspondence folder is its home.

9. "I'm drowning in open browser tabs with lesson ideas"

Give tabs to the AI: "for each link I paste, summarize in one sentence and file under subject." Then close the tabs. Lessons don't need to live in tabs; they need to live in your weekly loop's queue. Ten pasted links, ten sentences, one organized note, tabs closed, sleep.

10. "I don't trust any of it anymore"

Totally normal, and chapter 05's triage table is built exactly for the aftermath of discovering an error. The trust calibration to aim for: use generated material freely for structure (outlines, instructions, formats, prompts-for-self) and treat every fact as a rumor until you've seen a source. That calibration is what the whole guide teaches — and after a month it's as automatic as checking a receipt.

Still stuck?

  • Re-read the chapter for the area that's failing — each one ends with its failure and its fix built in.
  • Check the prompt you actually pasted. Most "the AI can't do this" moments are "the prompt had no context" moments.
  • The issues page of this project's repository exists for guide bugs; real classroom additions are welcome.

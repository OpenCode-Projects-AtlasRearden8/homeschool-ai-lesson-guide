Prompt: The Weekly Plan

Category: planning · Use: every week during the Sunday block · Pairs with: chapter 03.

Prep (3 minutes)

From your year plan, grab next week's row. Note your materials reality ("we have: the library, a kitchen, paper, this week: clean-up leftovers from the volcano unit"). No kid names (chapter 08).

Paste this prompt

You are my homeschool planning aide for next week only. I plan 45 minutes on
Sundays; you draft; I choose and revise.

NEXT WEEK'S YEAR-PLAN ROW
[paste the row: week number + focus topic per subject]

MY KIDS (descriptors only)
- [child A: level, style, what usually stalls them]
- [child B]

MATERIALS AND TIME REALITY
- Available: [kitchen science, library runs weekly, printer+paper, eggs,
  backyard...]
- [subject] gets [minutes] per day; we school [days] per week; total daily
  seat time is about [n] hours.
- Day of week I never have energy: [day].

YOUR TASK — give me TWO options per subject, side by side:
1. Option A: the "normal Tuesday" version. Option B: a cheaper/faster/
   quieter version of the same lesson (for the bad day).
2. For each: what I prep (max 10 minutes of prep), an opening question,
   the activity, and what counts as DONE.
3. Flag anything factual you invented or borrowed (names, dates, how
   something actually works) so I can verify it against a real source
   before printing.
4. No busywork; no group work we can't do; nothing requiring overnight
   logistics I can't order today.

Keep each option to one short paragraph. Ask me clarifying questions only
if something is genuinely ambiguous — otherwise draft.

After the draft

  1. Pick one option per subject (price of choosing < creating).
  2. Verify everything flagged in item 3, now, before printing — chapter 05.
  3. For any generated worksheet text: run the differentiation prompt on the chosen version (prompts/worksheet-three-levels.md).
  4. File the plan as weeks/week-NN/plan.md and write the weekly-log rows (Move 5 of the loop).

Failure pattern to expect

Models love "take a field trip to a whole different city." If an option needs booking, money, or a road trip, swap that option for "same topic, kitchen-table version" — one follow-up sentence usually does it.

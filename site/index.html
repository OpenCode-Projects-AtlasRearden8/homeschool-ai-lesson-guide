The Homeschool AI Lesson Guide

what this guide is, what you need, and the fastest honest path through it. Time to read: 10 minutes.

A plain-English manual for parent-teachers: plan a year of lessons with AI as co-planner, generate differentiated worksheets at reading levels, fact-check everything, keep portfolios and logs, and find your state's homeschooling paperwork requirements — without legal advice. Warm, never condescending, local-first.

This is the parent-teacher running a full year at home guide, deliberately distinct from the classroom-teacher pack and the one-on-one tutoring guide in the same family of AI guides from Asdale Dynamics, LLC.

The manual

  • Start Herewhat this guide is, what you need, and the fastest honest path through it. Time to read: 10 minutes.
  • Principlesyou are the teacher; the AI is an aide with no teaching license and no knowledge of your kid. Time to do: 10 minutes of reading, nothing to set up.
  • Plan Your Yearbuild a 36-week plan from your subject list, your curriculum, and your real constraints — using the AI as a structuring partner, not an oracle. Time to do: about 60–90 minutes, once per year.
  • The Weekly Loopone 45-minute planning block each week that turns next week's row on the year plan into actual, printable, differentiated materials. Time to do: 45 minutes per week, forever after.
  • Differentiated Worksheetsturn one lesson into three worksheets — below level, on level, above level — in about 10 minutes with a structured prompt. Time to do: 10 minutes per lesson per subject per level.
  • Fact-Check EverythingAI-generated text is confident even when wrong. Here's a 5-minute per-lesson verification routine that catches almost everything, without turning you into a full-time editor. Time to do: 5 minutes per lesson.
  • Portfolios and Logsone records tree, two documents you update weekly, and a quarterly 30-minute review. Your future self — and, if requested, your state — can reconstruct the whole year in an afternoon. Time to set up: 30 minutes once.
  • State Paperwork — Informationally, Never Legal Advicehow to find your own state's homeschool requirements from official sources, organize them into your records system, and know when to ask a professional instead of trusting a chatbot — or this guide. Time to do: 20 minutes, once, plus 10 to re-verify annually.
  • Privacy and Local AIwhat never goes into a cloud prompt, plus a realistic look at running AI on your own machine. Time to do: 10 minutes reading; local setup optional and its own evening project.
  • Low Energy and AccommodationsADHD-friendly and low-spoons adaptations for both sides of the desk — the teaching parent and the learning kid. Time to do: read now, steal what works.
  • Troubleshootingthe ten failure patterns you'll actually hit, and the fastest honest fix for each. Bookmark this one.

Templates — copy into your records folder

  • Accommodations Notes — [child descriptor]A two-page private file describing what works, without diagnoses (chapter 08) — for you, for any evaluator, and for future-you re-deriving what worked last year. Never sent to cloud AI except in descriptor form.
  • Portfolio Index — [year]The table of contents of your homeschool year — what an evaluator, a future school, or tired-you-at-the-end reads first (chapter 06). Update monthly; it's the same sitting as the quarterly review.
  • State Paperwork Checklist — [STATE]Filled in by YOU from YOUR state's official pages (chapter 07). Not legal advice; a record of what you found and where. Re-verify each school-start season and after every move.
  • Weekly Log — 2026-2027One file, all year. Five minutes each Sunday during the planning block (chapter 03, Move 5). Evidence column filled the same night — that habit is the whole portfolio.
  • Year Plan — 2026-2027Copy this file, rename to `year-plan.md`, fill in. Chapter 02 explains how the AI helps build this; the five-facts prep list lives there.
